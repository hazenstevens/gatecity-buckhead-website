The Bible teaches that death is not the end. When a person who trusts in Jesus dies, they go to be with the Lord. Paul called it being "absent from the body" and "at home with the Lord" (2 Corinthians 5:8). But that is not the final chapter. One day Jesus will return, raise the dead with real, imperishable bodies, and make heaven and earth new. Heaven is real, and its greatest gift is not the scenery. It is God Himself, living with His people forever. Scripture is also honest that there is a real separation from God for those who refuse His mercy, which is why the question of what happens when you die matters so much today.

Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will still live, even if he dies. Whoever lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?”

John 11:25-26, World English Bible

Is Heaven Real?

Yes. Jesus spoke of His Father's house and promised His followers, "I am going to prepare a place for you" (John 14:2). But many of us carry a picture of heaven that is smaller than the Bible's.

I will never forget hearing a teacher challenge the cartoon version of heaven: chubby angels, floating clouds and harps. Scripture describes something far more solid and far more beautiful. The final hope of the gospel is not that we escape the world, but that God redeems it. Heaven comes to earth.

I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth have passed away, and the sea is no more. I saw the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared like a bride adorned for her husband. I heard a loud voice out of heaven saying, “Behold, God’s dwelling is with people, and he will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God. He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more; neither will there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain, any more. The first things have passed away.”

Revelation 21:1-4, World English Bible

The Best Part of Heaven Is God

At the center of eternity is not only the absence of pain but the presence of God. John saw a city with no temple, "for the Lord God, the Almighty, and the Lamb, are its temple" (Revelation 21:22). There is no need for the sun, because the glory of God gives it light (Revelation 22:5). Every barrier that once separated us from Him is gone. That is the pinnacle of Christian hope: unhindered communion with the One who made us and loves us.

The Resurrection of the Body

Eternal life begins the moment we put our faith in Jesus. Jesus told Nicodemus that entering God's kingdom requires being born again (John 3:3). But the promise is not only spiritual. It is physical. Christianity teaches the resurrection of the body.

Behold, I tell you a mystery. We will not all sleep, but we will all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we will be changed. For this perishable body must become imperishable, and this mortal must put on immortality.

1 Corinthians 15:51-53, World English Bible

The same Jesus who walked out of His tomb, ate fish with His friends and showed them His scars will one day raise everyone who belongs to Him. Death, the ancient enemy, will be swallowed up in victory.

Rewards That Last

Jesus told us to store up treasure in heaven rather than in things that rust and fade (Matthew 6:19-20). The New Testament speaks of crowns for those who persevere in trials (James 1:12) and long for Christ's appearing (2 Timothy 4:8). Even a cup of cold water given in Jesus' name will not be forgotten (Matthew 10:42). What you do in faith and love today echoes into eternity.

The Sobering Truth

The gospel is a message of hope, but it is also a warning. Jesus spoke plainly about judgment and about outer darkness for those who reject God's grace (Matthew 25:30). John saw a great white throne where all people will stand before God (Revelation 20:11-15). I do not share this to frighten anyone into faith. I share it because it is true, and because love tells the truth.

The good news is that no one has to face that day alone or unforgiven.

For God so loved the world, that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

John 3:16, World English Bible

Living in Light of Eternity

Knowing what happens after death changes how we live before it:

  • It gives us endurance. Our present troubles are "light" and "for the moment" compared with the eternal glory ahead (2 Corinthians 4:17).
  • It calls us to holiness. We live as people who will one day see Him face to face (2 Peter 3:11-12).
  • It sends us on mission. We plead with others, on Christ's behalf, to be reconciled to God (2 Corinthians 5:20).

If you are grieving someone you love, hold on to this: for those who are in Christ, death is not goodbye. It is "see you soon."
