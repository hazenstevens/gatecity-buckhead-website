Formed for a Purpose, Part 3. Key text: 1 Timothy 3.

The face that tells us who we are

In the 1970s, Dr. Edward Tronick began a simple experiment. A parent sits face to face with their baby, smiling, talking, responding. The baby lights up. Then the parent goes still. Same parent, same room, but the face goes blank. Within seconds the child begins reaching, pointing, crying, trying everything to win back that connection. When the parent re-engages, the child settles.

We were made to receive our identity from a face that is turned toward us. Some of us received that from a natural father. Some of us did not. But every one of us was made to receive it from our heavenly Father. “The Lord make His face shine upon you” (Numbers 6:25). Before God ever asks anything of us, He turns His face toward us.

That order matters. The call of this series is to be formed for a purpose, and this week the purpose is to serve. But you cannot serve well from a blank face.

Jesus served from knowing

John 13 holds one of the most beautiful sentences in Scripture:

“Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into His hands, and that He had come from God and was going to God, rose from supper and laid aside His garments, took a towel and girded Himself.” (John 13:3-4)

Jesus knew where He came from. He knew where He was going. And because He knew, He could lay aside His garment and take the posture of a servant. Security came first. Then the towel.

Identity before service, not identity in place of service.

Sons who serve

A quick word on sonship. In Scripture, sonship is not about gender. It describes a person entrusted with inheritance, responsibility, and authority. “You are no longer a slave but a son, and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ” (Galatians 4:7). Every daughter and every son in God’s family carries that sonship.

We see this pattern in Paul and Timothy. Paul does not write to Timothy as an employer to a staff member. He writes as a father to a son:

  • “To Timothy, my true son in the faith” (1 Timothy 1:2)
  • “This charge I commit to you, son Timothy” (1 Timothy 1:18)
  • “To Timothy, a beloved son... greatly desiring to see you, being mindful of your tears” (2 Timothy 1:2, 4)
  • “Timothy, who is my beloved and faithful son in the Lord” (1 Corinthians 4:17)

Then Paul says this to a whole church, out loud:

“I have no one like-minded, who will sincerely care for your state. For all seek their own, not the things which are of Christ Jesus. But you know his proven character, that as a son with his father he served with me in the gospel.” (Philippians 2:20-22)

Two small Greek words make this shine. The word for “like-minded” is isopsychos, literally “equal-souled.” And the word for “sincerely” is gnēsiōs, from the same root Paul uses when he calls Timothy his “true” son, gnēsios. A genuine son became a genuinely caring servant. Timothy’s care for the church came out of his sonship.

That is the testimony we should want. Not “he has a great platform” or “she has a big gift,” but “I have no one else like them. They genuinely care. They served with me as a son with his father.” That is what you want your leaders to be able to say about you.

And because Paul knew Timothy was secure, he was not afraid to call him to hard things. Wage the good warfare (1 Timothy 1:18). Fight the good fight of faith (1 Timothy 6:12). Be a good servant, a diakonos, of Jesus Christ (1 Timothy 4:6). Endure hardship as a good soldier, compete like an athlete, labor like the hardworking farmer (2 Timothy 2:3-6). Sons can hear that kind of language without being crushed by it.

Two ditches

In our early season with God, so much of what He does is affirm our identity. He wants us to know His love. As we mature, He entrusts us with more responsibility in the kingdom. And on either side of that road there is a ditch.

The first ditch is when responsibility slowly replaces sonship. We start serving out of duty, pressure, or a religious compulsion to prove ourselves. The older brother in Luke 15 said, “All these years I have been serving you.” The father answered, “Son, you are always with me.” When we land here, the answer is to return and renew our first love (Revelation 2:4-5).

The second ditch is when we never move past wanting affirmation. We stay self-focused, always needing the next word, the next encouragement, the next platform. Philippians 2:21 names it: “all seek their own.” And 1 Timothy 3:6 warns about being “puffed up with pride.” Affirmation was never meant to be the destination. It was meant to be the foundation.

So we live in the tension as servant sons: sons in service to our heavenly Father, and sons in service to His church.

Go and be great

Jesus redefined greatness: “Whoever desires to become great among you shall be your servant” (Mark 10:43-45). “He who is greatest among you shall be your servant” (Matthew 23:11).

That is the heart of 1 Timothy 3. Paul says that if anyone desires the position of an overseer, “he desires a good work” (verse 1). Not a good title. A good work. And the word for deacon, diakonos, simply means servant.

A few phrases in the chapter amplify this:

  • Verse 5, “take care of the church of God.” The same word, epimeleomai, describes the Good Samaritan, who “took care of him” (Luke 10:34-35). Leadership is hands-on care.
  • Verse 13, those who serve well “obtain for themselves a good standing and great boldness in the faith.” Serving well is the path to greatness and boldness.
  • Verse 15, “the house of God.” The church is a household, a family, which brings us right back to sons and fathers.

And where does that service begin? At home. “If a man does not know how to rule his own house, how will he take care of the church of God?” (verse 5). If you desire leadership in the church, the first place the Bible directs you to work is your own home, not chasing platforms or opportunities. You serve God by serving your family first.

A commissioning

If you desire to lead, start here. Return to the Father’s face, again and again. Let His affirmation settle you. Plant yourself in a church family where that affirmation is echoed by spiritual mothers and fathers, and where service is understood as an extension of fidelity and love, and of faithfulness to God and to His church.

Then lay aside your garment. Pick up the towel. Serve at home first, then serve His house.

We serve from sonship. Identity before service, not identity in place of service.

Go and be great.
