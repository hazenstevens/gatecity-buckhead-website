Consider for a moment: when was a time you felt chosen? Not just included, not just tolerated — actually wanted. And what did it do in you?

I can still name mine. The first girl picked for dodgeball in primary school. A full-ride scholarship for college. The U-23 rugby national team at the end of my playing career.

Different seasons, same quiet answer to the question I was always half-asking underneath everything: do I have a place here? And when that question gets settled, something shifts. We stop striving to secure our spot and start living from it.

That's exactly where Paul begins in Ephesians.

Where Paul Starts

Ephesians has a great deal to say about how we live together and relate to one another — husbands and wives, parents and children, the whole household of faith. But before Paul gets to any of that, he starts somewhere more foundational: our relationship with God and our place in His family.

"For he chose us in him before the creation of the world to be holy and blameless in his sight. In love he predestined us for adoption to sonship through Jesus Christ…" — Ephesians 1:4–5

Before we can understand how to live well in our natural or spiritual families, we have to understand who we are in God's family. Paul tells us we have been chosen and adopted as His sons and daughters.

And being secure in that place gives us three things: an identity, a responsibility, and an inheritance.

1. In the Family, We Receive an Identity

Most of us wouldn't say out loud, "I don't know who I am." But we tend to discover where our identity is actually rooted when something important to us gets shaken.

When work isn't going well. When our children are struggling. When a relationship changes. When someone else gets the opportunity we wanted. When we fail at something we thought we were good at. Even when we go through a season where we don't feel particularly close to God.

Those moments have a way of revealing how much we've been leaning on a role, a relationship, an accomplishment, or someone else's approval to tell us we're okay.

Which is why Paul's words land the way they do: He chose us in him before the creation of the world. Before any of those roles existed. Before you accomplished a single thing. Before you had anything at all to offer God — He chose you.

Our identity as sons and daughters isn't something we have to keep achieving or defending. It's something we receive from the Father.

Maybe becoming secure in God's family begins with letting that move from something we know is true to the place we actually live from — the ground under our feet on ordinary Tuesdays, not just the truth we reach for when everything falls apart.

2. In the Family, We Receive a Responsibility

Paul says God chose us "to be holy and blameless in his sight." Notice the order. God chooses us first, and then He forms us.

When we're secure in our place in the family, obedience stops being about proving ourselves. The question changes. Instead of am I doing enough?, we start asking, what does it look like to live like my Father? We learn His ways — His forgiveness, His generosity, His patience, His honesty, His love.

And maturity begins to shift our focus from "What am I getting from this family?" to "What is my responsibility to this family?"

That shift shows up at home and it shows up at church. In both places, the principle is the same: we don't take responsibility so that we can belong. We take responsibility because we belong.

3. In the Family, We Receive an Inheritance

Paul also tells us we've been adopted through Jesus, and all through Scripture adoption is tied to inheritance. Romans 8 puts it plainly: "If we are children, then we are heirs — heirs of God and co-heirs with Christ."

Knowing there is an inheritance changes how we relate to the Father and to each other. We don't have to live from scarcity — constantly measuring what we have against what someone else has, worrying we've been overlooked, wondering whether God will actually come through for us.

There is enough in our Father's house.

Our inheritance includes His presence, His Spirit, His promises, His family, and the hope we have in Christ. When we're secure in that, we can hold things more loosely. We can genuinely celebrate what God is doing in someone else. We can trust that our Father knows what we need.

Bringing It Together

You don't need to have all of this figured out. Part of formation is simply becoming more aware of what we believe and where God may be inviting us to grow.

So sit with it this week. Maybe He's reminding you of who you are. Maybe He's inviting you to take greater responsibility as part of His family. Or maybe He's asking you to trust His goodness more deeply — to believe there really is enough in His house for you.

Wherever you find yourself, it all begins from the same place:

You have been chosen. You are loved. And you have a place in the family of God.

A few questions to carry with you:

  • When something in your life gets shaken, what do you reach for to tell you that you're okay?

  • Where might God be inviting you to move from "What am I getting?" to "What's my responsibility?"

  • What would change this week if you truly believed there is enough in your Father's house?
