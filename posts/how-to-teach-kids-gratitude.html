You teach kids gratitude by helping them see God as the giver. Thankfulness is not mainly good manners. It grows when children remember what God has done, receive life as a gift, learn to thank Him on hard days as well as good ones, and let thankfulness shape how they live. Those four steps (remember, receive, rejoice, respond) make a simple path for a family or a classroom through the Thanksgiving season.

My little children, of whom I am again in travail until Christ is formed in you

Galatians 4:19, World English Bible

Kids are never too young to know God, hear His voice and walk with Him. Gratitude is one of the first places that formation shows up.

Week 1: Remember

Gratitude begins with remembering. We become ungrateful when we lose sight of what God has already done for us.

Praise Yahweh, my soul, and don’t forget all his benefits;

Psalm 103:2, World English Bible

Try this at home: Start a "remember jar." Each night at dinner, everyone names one thing God did for them that day, writes it on a slip of paper and drops it in. By Thanksgiving you will have a jar full of reasons to thank Him.

Week 2: Receive

Gratitude grows when we stop seeing life as something we are owed and start receiving it as a gift. Children notice quickly when something feels unfair. This week, help them notice how much has been given.

Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom can be no variation, nor turning shadow.

James 1:17, World English Bible

Try this at home: Walk through your house and play "Who gave us this?" Point to the food, the bed, a friend's drawing on the fridge. Every answer leads back to the Father who gives good gifts.

Week 3: Rejoice

We can say thank you to God even on hard days, not just good ones. That is one of the most important things a child can learn: God is still good when the day is not.

Rejoice always. Pray without ceasing. In everything give thanks, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus toward you.

1 Thessalonians 5:16-18, World English Bible

Try this at home: At bedtime, ask two questions: "What was hard today?" and "Where was God with you in it?" Then thank Him together for being near.

Week 4: Respond

Real gratitude shows up in how we live: generous, worshipful and centered on God.

And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to which also you were called in one body; and be thankful.

Colossians 3:15, World English Bible

Try this at home: Let your kids choose one way to give during Thanksgiving week: a card for a neighbor, a bag of groceries for someone in need, or a song of thanks sung together before the meal.

Church and Home Moving Together

Spiritual formation goes deepest when church and home move together. God told parents to talk about His words "when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise up" (Deuteronomy 6:7). A Sunday lesson plants a seed, and a dinner-table conversation waters it.
