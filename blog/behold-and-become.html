Behold and Become

By Hazen Stevens · November 22, 2026

Formed for a Purpose, Part 4. Key text: 1 Timothy 4.

Where we have been

There is a well known piece of research called the Still Face Experiment, led by Dr. Edward Tronick. A parent sits face to face with a baby, warm and engaged, and the child is delighted. Then the parent’s face goes blank. Within moments the child begins to unravel. The child reaches, points, cries out, and does anything to get that face back. When the warmth returns, the child settles.

We were made to live in the light of a face. That picture sits underneath this whole series, so before we open chapter 4, here is where we have been.

God forms our purpose under pressure. Paul left Timothy in Ephesus to confront false teachers (1 Timothy 1:3). It was in that pressure that Paul reminded Timothy who he was and what had been spoken over him. (Read Purpose Under Pressure.)

Our purpose will not be formed without prayer. Prayer is the catalyst in our personal lives, and God moves in the large events of history through the prayers of His people. Prayer is the constant place we press in to walk out our purpose, both personally and on the stage of world affairs.

To be great, we have to be willing to go low. Servant leadership is the foundation for how we engage the world around us. But we can only serve from the right identity. We serve from sonship. (Read We Serve from Sonship.)

This week we come to the inner life. Our inner life has to be diligently formed, because your influence and your purpose will never exceed the strength of your character. Paul returns to this with Timothy in chapter 4.

Beholding and becoming

Paul is about to use strong words: train, toil, devote, practice, persist. If we hear those words without the right starting place, discipline turns into striving. So we begin where formation begins.

“For God, who said, ‘Let light shine out of darkness,’ has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” (2 Corinthians 4:6)
“And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another.” (2 Corinthians 3:18)

We behold, and we become. The Father’s purpose is that we would be conformed to the image of His Son (Romans 8:29), and the way He does it is by letting us see Him. This is the old blessing of Numbers 6:25 fulfilled: the Lord makes His face shine upon us.

There is a mystery here, and I do not want us to neglect it. The shaping of our interior life flows from a place of communion with God. The diligence Paul calls Timothy to is meant to be informed by an inner life in which we see Christ and become more like Him. We see Him in the Word. We see Him as sons and daughters who live in the light of the Father’s countenance. In Christ, the Father’s face is turned toward us, and everything else in this chapter grows from there.

The challenge, and the larger story

Paul opens the chapter by returning to the challenge Timothy faced.

“But the Spirit says expressly that in later times some will fall away from the faith, paying attention to seducing spirits and doctrines of demons, through the hypocrisy of men who speak lies, branded in their own conscience as with a hot iron, forbidding marriage and commanding to abstain from foods which God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth. For every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving. For it is sanctified through the word of God and prayer.” (1 Timothy 4:1-5, WEB)

This is the definition of the challenge. In Ephesus there were teachers whose message sounded spiritual, with strict rules about marriage and food, but it came from consciences that had gone numb.

Notice how Paul frames it. He places Timothy’s situation inside the larger story. This is not only a problem in one city in the first century. It is a challenge the church will face until the return of Christ. And Paul’s answer is not first a strategy. It is a kind of person. In light of this, here is how we are meant to be formed.

1. Diligence in our devotional life

“Rather train yourself for godliness; for while bodily training is of some value, godliness is of value in every way, as it holds promise for the present life and also for the life to come.” (1 Timothy 4:7-8)

Paul reaches for the language of the athlete. Nobody drifts into strength. The false teachers had consciences that were seared. Timothy’s inner life was to be exercised. This is the hidden place of prayer and communion where, as Paul prayed for the Ephesians, we are strengthened with power through His Spirit in the inner man (Ephesians 3:16).

And notice why we train. “To this end we toil and strive, because we have our hope set on the living God” (1 Timothy 4:10). We do not train to earn His face. We train because we have seen it.

2. Commitment to the Word of God

Paul tells Timothy that a good servant of Christ Jesus is one who is “trained in the words of the faith and of the good doctrine” (1 Timothy 4:6). Then he gets practical: “Until I come, devote yourself to the public reading of Scripture, to exhortation, to teaching” (1 Timothy 4:13).

The answer to false teaching is not suspicion. It is saturation in what is true. And the Word is more than our defense. It is where we behold Him. We open the Scriptures to see the face of Christ, and as we see Him we are changed.

3. A life that sets the example

“Let no one despise you for your youth, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity.” (1 Timothy 4:12)

This is the key. Timothy’s authority in Ephesus was not going to come from his age or his position. It was going to come from his life.

Someone once shared a story with me. They were in a work situation with an adversarial person, someone who was convinced my friend was gunning for their job. As they worked through that circumstance, the person finally asked, “How are you able to maintain such a positive disposition in life?” They even confessed to being a little bit jealous of it.

My friend answered honestly. They said they were actually a pretty anxious person, someone who had really struggled to be those things, and that it was the work Christ was doing in their life that allowed them to live that way. It was a sincere and honest response, and it led to a further conversation about that person’s own faith upbringing.

That is what Paul means. “Keep a close watch on yourself and on the teaching. Persist in this, for by so doing you will save both yourself and your hearers” (1 Timothy 4:16). The outflow of the interior life is a formation that reaches people. This is why we say at GateCity that we are Christ formed and on mission. The formed life is the mission field’s first sermon.

Practice these things

“Practice these things, immerse yourself in them, so that all may see your progress.” (1 Timothy 4:15)

Paul does not say so that all may see your perfection. He says progress. Your influence and your purpose will never exceed the strength of your character, and character is formed one day at a time in the light of His face.

So this week, behold Him. Give yourself to the secret place. Open the Word to see Christ. And let the life He is forming in you become the example that someone near you is waiting to see.

Behold and become.
