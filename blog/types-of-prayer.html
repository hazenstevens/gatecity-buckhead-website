Types of Prayer in the Bible: Worship, Lament, Honest Anger and Silence

By Hazen Stevens · November 10, 2026

In this post: What are the types of prayer? · Is it okay to lament or be angry in prayer? · How to pray when you have no words

Adapted from Chapter 6 of our book, Conversations.

The Bible shows many types of prayer, and each one fits a different season of the soul. There is worship, when prayer sounds like praise. There is lament, when prayer sounds like grief. There is honest anger, when prayer sounds like a fight. And there is silence, when prayer has no words at all. Alongside these sit thanksgiving, confession, petition and intercession. God is not looking for one prayer tone. He is looking for honesty.

You don't talk to a friend the same way you talk to a doctor. You don't use the same tone when you are laughing as when you are grieving. Prayer is the same. Our conversations with God shift with what we are walking through, and all of it belongs.

1. Worship: When Prayer Sounds Like Praise

Worship is more than singing. It is declaring who God is, often in contrast to how we feel or what we see. One of the most repeated lines in the whole Bible is this:

Give thanks to Yahweh, for he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever.

Psalms 107:1, World English Bible

When King Jehoshaphat faced an army far too big to defeat, God told him to put worshipers at the front of the army (2 Chronicles 20:21). As they sang, the Lord set ambushes against their enemies. Worship was not the response after the victory. It was the pathway to it.

And worship is the steady rhythm of heaven. Around God's throne, day and night, they never stop saying "Holy, holy, holy" (Revelation 4:8). Jesus Himself gave thanks before feeding the five thousand and again at the Last Supper, knowing the cross was coming.

Try it: Begin your prayer by naming who God is. "You are faithful. You are with me. You are holy." Don't wait for a good mood to praise God. Let worship lead you there.

2. Lament: When Prayer Sounds Like Grief

Sometimes the only honest prayer is a painful one. Nearly a third of the Psalms are laments. These are not prayers of doubt. They are acts of defiant trust, turning toward God in pain instead of away from Him.

How long, Yahweh? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?

Psalms 13:1, World English Bible

Jesus prayed a lament on the cross, quoting Psalm 22: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Matthew 27:46). He wept over Jerusalem (Matthew 23:37). Jeremiah wrote an entire book of lament, and even there, hope flickers:

This I recall to my mind; therefore I have hope. It is because of Yahweh’s loving kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his compassion doesn’t fail. They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:21-23, World English Bible

Hannah went through a season of severe physical pain that brought her prayers down to a whisper: "Help me, God. Just help me through the next moment." Around three in the morning one night, she sensed God inviting her to let go of needing to know how long it would last. "I don't need to know how long," she finally prayed. "I just need to know You're here." The pain did not vanish right away, but a comfort came that she says nothing in this world could match.

Try it: Write your own psalm. Start with the complaint: "God, where are You?" Name what is breaking you. Then, if you can, end with a small whisper of trust: "But I trust in Your unfailing love."

3. Honest Anger: When Prayer Sounds Like a Fight

Yes, even anger can be prayer. Job cried out that he called for help and got no justice (Job 19:7). Jeremiah told God, "You have persuaded me, and I was persuaded" (Jeremiah 20:7). Psalm 88 ends with no resolution at all. And God kept these prayers in His Word. That is His invitation to bring everything, even the parts you think you are not allowed to say.

The opposite of love is not anger. It is apathy. If you are still talking to God in your frustration, you are still in relationship.

Try it: Tell God the truth without dressing it up. "I don't understand this. I feel let down." Fight toward God, not away from Him.

4. Silence: When Prayer Has No Words

There are seasons when the words will not come. In those moments, silence is not a failure in prayer. It is prayer.

“Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted in the earth.”

Psalms 46:10, World English Bible

In the same way, the Spirit also helps our weaknesses, for we don’t know how to pray as we ought. But the Spirit himself makes intercession for us with groanings which can’t be uttered.

Romans 8:26, World English Bible

When I first tried quiet, contemplative prayer, my mind raced from my to-do list to what I might have for lunch. A friend leading our group told us, "The goal isn't to empty yourself, but to create space for God to fill." Week after week, the noise slowly settled, and I began to experience real stillness with God, like sitting with a dear friend where words are not always needed.

Try a breath prayer: A breath prayer is a short phrase of Scripture prayed in rhythm with your breathing, something Christians have done for centuries. Breathe in: "Be still." Breathe out: "and know that I am God." Or breathe in: "Jesus, Son of God." Breathe out: "have mercy on me." Five minutes, no phone, no music. Just sit with Him.

God Meets You in Every Type of Prayer

These are not categories to master. They are movements of the soul, and every one is welcome in God's presence. Your prayers won't always be neat, emotional or clear. But if they are honest, they are holy. The point isn't to say the right thing. The point is to stay in the conversation.
