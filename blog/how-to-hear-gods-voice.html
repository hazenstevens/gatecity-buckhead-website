How to Hear God's Voice: Prayer as a Two-Way Conversation

By Hazen Stevens · November 10, 2026

In this post: How do I hear God's voice? · Does God still speak today? · Five simple practices for listening

Adapted from Chapter 3 of our book, Conversations.

You hear God's voice by treating prayer as a conversation instead of a speech. God speaks most often through Scripture, through the quiet prompting of the Holy Spirit, through other believers, and through circumstances that line up with what He has already said. Hearing Him starts with believing He wants to talk with you, then slowing down long enough to listen.

Ask almost anyone, "Have you ever prayed?" and most will say yes. Even people who do not consider themselves religious reach out to God in a crisis. But ask, "Do you hear God's voice?" and most people pause. For many, prayer feels like a message sent into the sky with no idea if it was received. That gap between saying prayers and actually hearing God is one of the reasons we wrote this book.

God Has Always Been a Speaking God

From the first page of Scripture, God speaks. He spoke to Abraham under the stars, to Moses from a burning bush, to the prophets in visions and dreams, and to Elijah in a quiet whisper. Jesus said His followers would know His voice.

My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.

John 10:27, World English Bible

‘Call to me, and I will answer you, and will show you great and difficult things, which you don’t know.’

Jeremiah 33:3, World English Bible

Moses knew this kind of relationship. Scripture says the Lord would speak to him face to face, the way a person talks with a friend (Exodus 33:11). And the New Testament says the covenant we now live under is built on better promises than the one Moses knew (Hebrews 8:6). What looks rare in the Old Testament is meant to be normal for every child of God.

Jesus modeled this perfectly. He lived in constant conversation with the Father and said He only did what He saw the Father doing (John 5:19). Then He promised that the Holy Spirit would guide us into all truth (John 16:13).

The Moment Prayer Became a Conversation for Me

I grew up believing prayer mattered, but I never expected God to answer back. Prayer was something I did toward God, not something He did with me.

That changed in my early college years. I was at a low point. Outwardly I had a good path ahead of me, but inside I felt lost. So I prayed differently than I ever had, not with polished words but with raw honesty. I needed God to speak.

Then I started noticing that the Scriptures I read seemed to speak directly to the questions in my heart. Later that same day, the same passage would come up in a conversation, a sermon or a song. At first I called it coincidence. But the pattern kept happening. God was speaking, guiding and responding, and I could not ignore it. Prayer stopped being a monologue and became a conversation.

You Can Call Him Father

Here is the heart of it. If you have put your faith in Jesus, you have received His Spirit, and you have been adopted into God's family.

For you didn’t receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry, “Abba! Father!” The Spirit himself testifies with our spirit that we are children of God;

Romans 8:15-16, World English Bible

"Abba" was the word a small child used for their dad. Jesus taught us to pray "Our Father in heaven" (Matthew 6:9): intimate and near, yet holy and all-powerful. You are not a servant trying to get the attention of a distant king. You are a child talking with a Father who wants to talk with you.

How to Hear God's Voice: Five Simple Practices

1. Start with Scripture. God's Word is the clearest place He speaks, and everything else we sense must line up with it. Read slowly. When a verse stops you, pause and ask, "Lord, what are You saying to me here?"

2. Listen as much as you talk. The South African pastor Andrew Murray, whose book on prayer Hannah and I read together early in our marriage, put it this way: prayer is not monologue but dialogue, and God's voice in response to ours is its most essential part. After you speak, sit quietly for a few minutes.

3. Notice the patterns. God often confirms what He is saying through more than one source: a verse, a sermon, a friend's word, a song. When the same truth keeps finding you, pay attention.

4. Test what you hear. God's voice will never contradict His Word or His character. It leads toward Jesus, toward love and toward obedience. Share what you sense with mature believers and let them help you weigh it.

5. Trust Him and take the next step. "Trust in Yahweh with all your heart, and don't lean on your own understanding" (Proverbs 3:5). Hearing God grows as we respond to what He has already said.

A Relationship, Not a Skill

Jesus said we are branches and He is the vine, and apart from Him we can do nothing (John 15:5). Conversation with God is not an extra feature of the Christian life. It is the lifeline. There will be seasons when prayer flows easily and seasons when it feels like a struggle. But God is not waiting to be impressed by perfect words. He is a Father who wants your presence more than your performance.

Try it today. Open your Bible, read a short passage, and then ask Him one honest question. Then wait. He is already speaking.
